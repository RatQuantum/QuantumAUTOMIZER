## Day 2: two full episodes of "Why we do what we do" (Seedance 2.0)

### #2 · Why do we hang pots high on Janmashtami? (Dahi Handi) · refs: krishna-child, yashoda, gopis, loc-gokul
1. Wide sunny village morning: girls hang clay pots of butter from a high rope between two houses and tie them tight,
   laughing, while a small blue-skinned boy watches from behind a cow, grinning.
2. Close: the little boy's eyes follow the pot swinging high above, he rubs his hands together, a peacock feather bobbing.
3. The boy whistles; five village boys run in from the lanes, and they climb on each other's shoulders into a wobbly
   human pyramid, the blue boy climbing to the top.
4. Low angle: the pyramid sways, the boy at the top stretches, taps the pot with a stick: it cracks and white butter and
   curd splash down on all the boys below, who burst out laughing.
5. The mother in the green sari arrives with her hands on her hips; the boy on top freezes with butter all over his face,
   then smiles the sweetest smile; she tries not to laugh and fails.
6. Insert (5 s): a butter-covered small blue hand licking butter off its fingers, close-up, slow motion.
7. Modern day (same style): a crowded street festival at night with colourful lights, a real human pyramid of young men
   reaching a clay pot hung high, the crowd cheering, water spraying.

### #3 · Why is Janmashtami at midnight? · refs: vasudeva, krishna-child (as a baby), loc-prison
1. Midnight storm, a stone prison: a father holds a newborn wrapped in yellow cloth, the oil lamp flickers, the guards
   asleep against the wall.
2. Magic: the iron chains fall from his wrists by themselves, the prison doors swing open silently, light spills in.
3. He places the baby in a woven basket on his head and walks out into the rain, lightning behind the city walls.
4. The river Yamuna in full flood under lightning; he steps in, the water rises to his chest, the basket held high.
5. A giant gentle many-hooded serpent rises from the water behind him and spreads its hoods over the basket like an
   umbrella; the rain stops on the baby.
6. Insert: the baby's tiny blue foot slips out of the basket and touches the water, and the flood calms and parts.
7. Modern day: a temple at midnight full of people, conch shells and lamps raised, a small cradle with flowers rocking.

### #4 · Why does Krishna wear a peacock feather? · refs: krishna, loc-gokul
1. A forest clearing at sunset: the young man with blue skin sits on a rock and plays a bamboo flute, eyes closed.
2. Peacocks gather from the trees, then start to dance in a circle around him, tails fully open, shimmering.
3. Slow motion: the peacocks' dance grows wild and joyful; leaves and petals swirl in the light.
4. The music ends; the king of the peacocks walks up and bows, and one perfect feather falls at his feet.
5. He picks up the feather, smiles, and tucks it into his hair; the peacocks call out.
6. Insert: the feather's eye in close-up catching golden light, slowly turning.
